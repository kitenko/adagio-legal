Adagio is an app that improves the smoothness of your videos. All video processing happens on your device. This policy describes what data the app processes, why, for how long, and what you can do about it.

In short

  • Your videos never leave your device. Not the originals, not the results, not frames, thumbnails or file names. We operate no server that could receive video, and there is no cloud processing.
  • There is no account. We do not ask for a sign-up, an e-mail address or a phone number, and we do not build a profile. If you choose to write to us, we receive only what you send.
  • We collect anonymous technical statistics about how the app performs — with no device, installation or user identifier. You can turn it off in Settings.
  • When the app crashes, it sends a technical crash report — with no video, file names, text or identifiers — to the Sentry service, whose servers are in the EU. These reports exist so we can find and fix crashes, and they have no switch.
  • Payments are handled by the app store, not by us. We never see your payment details.

Video and photo library access

Adagio only ever works with the video you pick yourself in the system picker.

  • The selected video is read and processed locally, on your device, using the device's own hardware (neural engine, video encoder).
  • The result is written to your device and, if you ask for it, to your photo library. On the Mac you have a second choice: save the finished video into a location you pick yourself in the system save panel. The app is granted write access only to what you picked there — not to the folder as a whole, and not to anything else.
  • None of this is transmitted to us or to any third party.
  • The app requests two photo library permissions: reading the video you selected, and saving the finished result.
  • On the Mac two file permissions are added, and both are bounded by your own choice: reading the file you picked in the open panel, and writing the finished video where you picked in the save panel. Without your choice the app reads and writes nothing.
  • A third and final one is the notification permission. The system asks for it once: after your first finished video, or when you press “Turn on” in the Privacy section yourself. It exists for exactly one message — that a video is ready; the notification itself is assembled on the device and is never sent anywhere. Refusing breaks nothing: the app will not ask again, and iOS Settings can turn it back on.
  • Nothing else is requested: the app has no camera and no microphone access — Adagio cannot record video, it can only import it. Location, contacts and calendar are not used.
  • If you choose to share a result, the app hands the file to the system share sheet. Where it goes from there is your decision; we are not told.

What is stored on the device itself

Inside the app's own protected storage:

  • references to the clips you selected and to the results you created (file paths, poster images, duration, resolution, processing settings);
  • editing drafts and the processing job queue, so work is not lost across a restart;
  • the count of free results you have used and whether Pro was purchased. That counter is a single integer plus a schema version number; it contains no device identifier.
  • three housekeeping markers about the app's own state — whether onboarding has been completed, whether anything has ever been imported on this device, whether a language was chosen manually. Each is a single yes/no value or a short word, with no identifiers and nothing about your videos. Like the free-result counter, these outlive the deletion of your clips: the app remembers that the library was once not empty so that, after you clear it, it can tell you it removed only its own copies.
  • a technical diagnostics log of video processing — timestamped technical events (for example, which stage started or failed and with which settings). File paths, identifiers and error texts are removed before anything is written. It holds at most 1,000 lines (256 KB); older lines are dropped.

None of this is transmitted anywhere. The diagnostics log leaves the device only if you export it yourself (Settings → "Export diagnostics") and choose where to send it. All of it is deleted together with the app, with two exceptions — both live in the system keychain, are transmitted nowhere, and are removed when the device itself is erased:

  • the free-result counter, so that reinstalling the app does not reset your free allowance; it holds nothing but a number and a schema version;
  • the purchase record, so the app recognises your purchase after a reinstall and support can find your order if you lose access to your store account. It holds the product identifier, the store's transaction identifier, the purchase environment (production or test), the ownership type and the purchase date. It holds no identifier of your store account.

Anonymous app performance statistics

What this is. The app sends aggregate technical measurements: how long processing took and how long the processed range was, how many frames were produced, at which quality mode and factor, which class the source video falls into (resolution class, frame-rate class, aspect ratio), which frame-size class the processing model was chosen for, how far the frame had to be shrunk, the device's thermal band during the work, how the job ended (success, failure, cancellation) and for which reason, on which platform and major OS version, plus anonymous counters of steps taken in the app and of actions on a result.

The complete list. Every measurement, every label on it and every value that label can take is published as a separate document: Exactly what Adagio sends. It is checked against the app's own measurement schema automatically, so it cannot fall behind the code.

What is explicitly NOT sent: video, frames or thumbnails; file names, paths or photo library identifiers; device, installation, user, account or advertising identifiers; exact resolution, duration or frame rate; EXIF data and geotags; capture timestamps; IP addresses and user agents as part of the statistics; error text, logs and stack traces as part of the statistics (a technical crash report works separately — see "Crash reports"); screenshots or session replay; the recipients you shared a result with; transaction identifiers, receipts and payment details.

Why. So we can select the processing model that suits the real capability of a class of devices, and so we can find release defects. This is not advertising and not profiling: these records cannot be linked back to you or to your device, because we store no identifier that would make that possible.

Legal basis. Our legitimate interest in keeping the app working and improving its quality (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR). The statistics are audience-measurement in nature: strictly anonymous, not combined with other data, and not shared with third parties for their own purposes.

Where it is processed. The data is received by a gateway and a monitoring system located in the European Union (Frankfurt, Germany). The statistics are not transferred outside the EU.

How to turn it off. Settings → “Anonymous metrics” (the same screen opens from Settings → Privacy Center → “What is sent”) → “Withdraw consent”; collection can be turned back on there too. Turning it off stops transmission immediately and erases anything still waiting to be sent from the device. The app keeps working in full — the statistics have no influence on video processing.

Crash reports

What this is. If the app terminates abnormally — or iOS closes it on screen for no reason it can explain, which usually means it ran out of memory — the app sends a technical crash report the next time it starts. On a Mac this includes an internal error of the app intercepted by the system: the app closes, and the report is sent the same way.

Besides crash reports, short notices of failures inside the app go to the same service:

  • an unhandled error — only the fact itself and in which of two parts of the program it occurred;
  • a failed operation — the area of the app (for example, import, processing, saving, purchase) and a reason from a predefined list of codes (for example, "timed out", "disk full", "file missing").

These notices contain no error text, file names, paths or identifiers — only these values and the technical fields of the Sentry service itself.

What a crash report contains (the complete list; everything else is removed on the device before sending):

  • the type of failure and how it was caught, the system signal code, and whether the failure was handled;
  • the state of the program at the moment of the crash: which functions of the app and of system libraries were running (addresses in program code and function names) and which thread crashed;
  • the names of program modules — the app itself and system libraries — with their build identifiers, load addresses and sizes;
  • the app version and build number, the build environment (for example, test), the operating system name, version and build number, the device model and family (for example, "iPhone", "Mac"), the processor architecture, whether it is a simulator, and whether the app was on screen;
  • the name and version of the Sentry library built into the app and the public key of our Sentry project;
  • a random report number, the time of the crash and a random launch number, which tie the report neither to you nor to your device.

What a report does not contain: video, frames or thumbnails; names of files or clips, or paths; error text or logs; screenshots; a history of actions in the app; device, installation, user or advertising identifiers. As with any request to a server, the device's network address is visible to Sentry when the report arrives, but storing it is prohibited in our organization's Sentry settings.

Who receives it. The Sentry service (Functional Software, Inc.) — our data processor under a data processing agreement. Reports are stored on Sentry's servers in the European Union (Frankfurt, Germany). Only we have access to them.

Why, and on what legal basis. To find and fix crashes. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in keeping the app working (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR). Reports are always sent and have no separate switch: a report is reduced to the minimum needed to find the cause of a crash and cannot reveal who you are or what you processed.

How long it is kept. 30 days, after which Sentry deletes the report.

How to object. You have the right to object to this processing (Article 21 GDPR) — write to adagio.app.support@gmail.com. Because reports carry no identifiers, we cannot locate the reports of a particular person; we will consider your objection and reply to you.

If you write to us

If you e-mail us — for support or with a question about your data — we receive your e-mail address, your name if your mail program shows it, and whatever you put in the message and its attachments — including the diagnostics log, if you choose to export and attach it. The app itself never sends us anything of this kind: a message exists only if you decide to write.

Why. Only to answer you and resolve your request. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in answering the people who write to us (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR); when the request concerns your rights under the GDPR, handling it is our legal obligation (Article 6(1)(c) GDPR).

How long it is kept. Correspondence is deleted within 30 days once 12 months have passed since the last message in it. You can ask us to delete it sooner.

Where. Mail arrives in a Gmail mailbox; Google processes it under its own privacy policy (see "Transfers outside the EU").

Please do not attach videos or other personal material — we do not need them to help you.

Purchases

Adagio offers a number of free results, after which saving and exporting is unlocked by a one-time Pro purchase.

  • The purchase is processed by the App Store (Apple) or Google Play (Google). Card details, payment data and your store account identifier are handled by the store, not by us, and are never passed to us.
  • What remains on the device is the count of free results used and the purchase record described above under on-device data — which includes the store's transaction identifier, the value support can use to find your order.
  • Purchase events do not appear in the anonymous statistics at all — no purchase counters, no currency, no amounts. The only purchase-related thing they can carry is the anonymous fact that a request to the store failed, with no detail of any kind.
  • Refund rules and purchase terms are set by the app store.

Children

Adagio is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect their data. The app has no accounts, no profiles and no social features.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to request access to your personal data, its rectification or erasure, restriction of processing, data portability, and to object to processing based on legitimate interest.

One limitation we are obliged to state plainly: we have no way to locate "your" records inside the statistics — they are anonymous by construction and contain no identifier tying a record to a person or a device. An access or erasure request for a specific record therefore cannot be executed by us. That is a consequence of not holding data about you, not a refusal. You may object to the collection of statistics at any time and without giving a reason, using the switch in Settings; that stops transmission immediately. An objection to crash reports is made by e-mail, as described under "Crash reports". The same limitation applies to crash reports: they carry no identifiers, so we cannot locate the reports of a particular person.

Correspondence you have sent us is the exception: we can find it by your e-mail address, so you can ask us for a copy of it or for its deletion at any time.

Everything that is personal to you stays on your device. It is deleted by an action of yours — deleting a clip or a result in the app (including deleting only the imported copy of a video, which leaves the finished video in place), deleting the file from your photo library, or deleting the app — and in one case by the app itself: a clip has exactly one finished video, so when you process it again, that clip's previous finished video is replaced and removed from the device. The app says so before the run starts, and anything you already saved to your photo library is untouched.

You also have the right to lodge a complaint with the data protection supervisory authority where you live.

Transfers outside the EU

The statistics are processed in the EU and are not transferred outside it. Crash reports are stored by Sentry in the EU; under Sentry's terms, the content of the reports does not leave the selected storage region. For auxiliary functions of its service (for example, e-mail notifications to us), Sentry uses sub-processors in the United States on the basis of the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and, where that does not apply, the EU Standard Contractual Clauses. E-mail you send us is received in Gmail; Google may process it outside the EU, including in the United States, under its own terms. Video is not transferred anywhere at all. Payments are processed by Apple and Google under their own privacy policies.

Changes to this policy

If the data we collect, the purposes or the retention periods change, we publish a new revision with a new effective date and state what changed. Changes that broaden data collection take effect no earlier than the publication of the corresponding revision.

Contact

For any question about this policy or about personal data, write to the address given under "Who is responsible for your data".

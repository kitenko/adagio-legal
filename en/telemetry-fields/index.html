Version: 1.4. Last updated: 25 September 2026.

Adagio processes video entirely on your device. So that this promise can be checked rather than taken on trust, this page publishes the complete list of the anonymous statistics the app sends — every measurement, every label on it, and every value that label can take. The list is finite: the app cannot send a measurement that is not on this page, and a new one cannot be added without being described here — that is verified automatically on every build.

Crash reports are not part of these statistics. After a crash the app sends a crash report the next time it starts, and failures inside the app are reported as short notices; both go to a separate service, and what they contain is described in the Crash reports section of the Privacy Policy.

This is a companion to the Privacy Policy, not a replacement.

What this data does not contain

None of the measurements below contains, or can contain:

  • video, frames or thumbnails;
  • file names, paths or photo-library object identifiers;
  • device, install, user, account or advertising identifiers;
  • the exact resolution, duration or frame rate of your video;
  • EXIF, geotags or capture time;
  • error text, logs, stack traces, screenshots or screen recordings;
  • the people you sent a result to;
  • transaction identifiers, receipts or payment details.

The records carry no device or user identifier. One upload holds the records of one device and is stored with one shared timestamp, so the records of one upload are linked to each other. Nothing in them links different uploads to each other or to you, and we do not attempt such matching.

Both histograms use these bucket bounds, in seconds: 1, 2, 5, 10, 30, 60, 180, 600, 1800, 7200 and “above”. A histogram record carries three numbers: how many observations were folded into it, their sum, and the per-bucket counts. Which has a consequence better said than left out: when a record has folded exactly one observation, its sum is that render's duration, to a fraction of a second. It is still a number with no name, file or identifier attached — but it is not “only a bucket”, and calling it only a bucket would be untrue.

The steps toward buying Pro

These values of the transition label describe the way from the free videos to a purchase. They carry no amounts, prices, currency, receipts or transaction identifiers.

  • free_video_spent_1, free_video_spent_2, free_video_spent_3 — a finished video took the first, second or third free video;
  • free_limit_reached — the free videos had run out: the result is available for comparison only;
  • offer_opened_result — the Pro offer was opened from such a result;
  • offer_opened_sidebar — the Pro offer was opened with the Pro button in the list of videos;
  • offer_opened_settings — the purchase screen was opened from Settings;
  • status_opened_from_offer — the "Purchase status" link was opened from the offer;
  • buy_completed, buy_pending, buy_cancelled, buy_failed — how the store's payment sheet ended after "Buy": purchase, awaiting approval, declined, error;
  • restore_tapped — "Restore purchase", or "Try again" after a failed check, was tapped and the app asked the store.

Two labels worth explaining

model_tier is not your video's resolution. The app keeps several processing models for different frame sizes and picks a suitable one; this label names the bucket that was picked. It cannot be used to recover the clip's resolution, let alone its content.

The value none means none of our models took part at all: the frames were computed by the system's own engine, built into recent versions of iOS and macOS. No bucket describes that work, so we say so plainly instead of substituting the bucket that would have been picked. none reveals no more than the rest: not the clip's resolution and not its content, only that the device runs a recent enough system — which os_major already says.

os_major is the major version number only (18, not 18.4.1). That is enough to see “renders fail on this OS version” and far too little to single out a device: hundreds of millions of devices share the same number.

Where it goes, and how long it is kept

A gateway and monitoring system in the European Union (Frankfurt, Germany) receive the data. The statistics are not transferred outside the EU.

WhatRetention
Technical detail of how a job ended14 days
Anonymous daily performance and usage aggregates, including the steps toward buying90 days
Monthly purchase and revenue aggregates from the store's reports (not from the app)12 months

How to turn it off

Settings → “Anonymous metrics” (or Settings → Privacy Center → “What is sent”) → “Withdraw consent”. Turning it off stops sending immediately and erases anything not yet sent from the device. Nothing about video processing changes.

Why this list can be trusted

It is not transcribed from the code by hand. The build compares this page against the measurement schema the app itself uses: if a measurement, a label or a new label value is added to the app without being described here, the check fails and that build is not shipped.
